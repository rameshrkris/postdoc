About

A few questions that keep me curious

Welcome, and thanks for visiting. I am a theoretical physicist interested in general relativity, black-hole perturbations, gravitational waves, and quantum field theory in curved spacetime. I hope one of the questions I am working on piques your interest. I enjoy understanding how spacetime geometry shapes the behavior of fields—and how we can tell which mathematical effects correspond to something physically observable.

My earlier work focused on gauge-invariant gravitational perturbations and gravitational-wave polarization in general relativity and modified gravity. I have also studied wave dynamics and normal modes in rotating wormholes. More recently, I have become interested in investigating exceptional points and their relation to black-hole quasinormal modes: how these unusual spectral degeneracies arise, and whether they leave a measurable signature in the response to an orbiting or plunging particle. I am also exploring perturbations of a two-black-hole spacetime. Some of these projects have already led to papers; others are still taking shape.

I have recently begun exploring another question that I find especially interesting: can we develop a systematic way to classify quantum vacuum states in curved spacetimes? I am looking at how horizons, causal structure, symmetries, observers, and boundary conditions might help organize the different notions of vacuum. This is an early-stage research direction, alongside my more developed work on gravitational perturbations and wave dynamics. I earned my Ph.D. in Theoretical Physics from Baylor University in 2026 under Dr. Gerald Cleaver and am an Affiliate Research Professor with Baylor's EUCOS Group.

Areas of research

Four connected directions in gravitational physics

These are four research directions I am pursuing at different stages, from published calculations to questions I am just beginning to formulate. They share an interest in how geometry shapes classical and quantum fields, but each has its own problems and methods.

01 / Spectral degeneracies

Exceptional Points and Black Hole QNMs

I am interested in investigating exceptional points and their relation to black-hole quasinormal modes. These are special spectral degeneracies where both the complex frequencies and their modes can coalesce. I want to understand not only the mathematical structure near such a point, but also what it might mean for the ringdown signal of a black hole.

In a scalar-field model on Kerr spacetime, I am studying how a particle can excite these resonances, using Green functions and their residues. One question I am exploring is whether interference in the source can weaken or even suppress a particular exceptional-point contribution. I would eventually like to understand possible connections to extreme-mass-ratio inspirals (EMRIs), though that is a longer-term direction rather than an established gravitational-wave result.

02 / Gravitational response

Black Hole Perturbation Theory

I have long been interested in how we describe small disturbances of spacetime without confusing physical effects with coordinate or gauge choices. That has led me to work on gauge-invariant quantities, gravitational-wave polarization, and the connection between perturbation equations and measurable wave response.

One project I am now developing looks at perturbations of two Schwarzschild black holes held apart by a strut. I am working through how the perturbation variables relate to familiar gravitational-wave quantities, how to calculate energy flowing into each horizon, and how to evolve the disturbances through horizon-penetrating coordinates. Several of these calculations are still in progress.

03 / Rotating geometries

Wormhole Normal Modes and Ringdowns

Another part of my research concerns waves in rotating wormhole spacetimes. I have studied normal modes in AdS-Teo backgrounds and am investigating quasinormal modes in asymptotically flat rotating Teo wormholes. An interesting difference from black holes is that outgoing waves can travel toward either of the two asymptotic regions.

I am particularly interested in what happens when a deformation of the geometry couples angular modes that would otherwise be independent. This turns the wave equation into a coupled spectral problem. I have also carried out separate time-domain calculations to see how a disturbed field oscillates and decays.

04 / Quantum fields and geometry

QFT in Curved Spacetime

One of my main new research interests is the classification of quantum vacuum states in curved spacetime. In flat spacetime, the vacuum is relatively familiar; in curved spacetime, the choice of vacuum can depend on the geometry, the observer, the causal structure, and the boundary conditions. I have recently been exploring whether these ingredients can be brought together into a more systematic geometric classification.

The idea is to revisit the foundational insights of quantum field theory in curved spacetime and ask how they might extend to rotating black holes, AdS geometries, wormholes, and other modern settings. I am starting by identifying the relevant geometric criteria and exploring Kerr–AdS as a possible case study. This is a research program in its formative stage, not a completed classification.

I am also interested in particle creation, quantum mode mixing, and the dynamical Casimir effect, topics that connect naturally with the question of how a quantum field responds to its spacetime background.

Active investigations

Current projects

Here are some of the specific projects behind those interests. Some have produced published results, while others are ongoing calculations or ideas I am working to develop.

Ongoing · black-hole spectroscopy

Exceptional points and black-hole response

Investigating quasinormal-mode splitting, resolvent residues, and source excitation near non-Hermitian spectral degeneracies. The central question is when a spectral effect becomes a physical response.

Developing · EMRI dynamics

Exceptional points and extreme-mass-ratio inspirals

Exploring particle-driven resonance excitation and suppression in Kerr-related models, with inspiral-to-plunge dynamics and gravitational waveform implications as longer-term goals.

Active · gravitational perturbations

Two Schwarzschild black holes with a strut

Developing a perturbative description of a two-black-hole background, including the relation of perturbation variables to physical observables, horizon fluxes, and horizon-penetrating time evolution.

Published + manuscript under review

Rotating Teo wormhole spectra

Coupled angular channels and normal modes in the rotating AdS-Teo geometry, followed by asymptotically flat scalar QNMs, spectral hybridization, and two-ended outgoing radiation.

Published · conceptual foundation

Gauge invariance and gravitational-wave polarization

Identifying physically meaningful gravitational-wave observables in linearized gravity and metric f(R) theories, including plane-wave gauge invariants and polarization structure.

Manuscript in preparation

Exceptional points in non-reciprocal oscillators

I use coupled-oscillator models to explore spectral degeneracies and stability in a setting where the calculations can be understood in detail.

Exploratory · QFT in curved spacetime

Classifying quantum vacuum states

I am developing the foundations for a geometric classification of quantum vacua, asking how horizons, causal structure, symmetries, observers, and boundary conditions distinguish possible states. Kerr–AdS is a prospective starting point.
